Load Balancing

Problem Description

The Load Balancing (LB) problem, also known as Work Apportionment (WA), aims to assign a set of tasks to a set of machines in a way that is robust to any one machine's failure. The problem is formulated as follows. Let \(M\) and \(N\) denote the set of tasks and the set of machines, respectively. For task \(i \in M\), only a subset of machines, denoted by \(N^i \subseteq N\), are accessible. Let binary variables \(y_j\) be 1 if machine \(j\) is used and 0 otherwise. Let variables \(x_{i j}\) denote the amount of workload from task \(i\) to machine \(j\). \( b_j \) enforces the capacity requirement for each machine. \( a_i \) is the workload of task i. \(M_{i j}\) is the maximum of the workload of task i and capacity of machine j. The apportionment is required to be robust to any one machine's failure. The objective is to minimize the total cost for processing all workloads. $$ \begin{array}{llr} \min _{x, y} & \sum_{j \in N} y_j & \\ \text { s.t. } & x_{i j} \leq M_{i j} y_j & \forall i \in M, \forall j \in N^i \\ & \sum_{i \in M: j \in N^i} x_{i j} \leq b_j & \forall j \in N \\ & \sum_{j \in N^i \backslash\left\{j^{\prime}\right\}} x_{i j} \geq a_i & \forall i \in M, \forall j^{\prime} \in N^i \\ & y_j \in\{0,1\} & \forall j \in N \\ & 0 \leq x_{i j} \leq b_j & \forall i \in M, \forall j \in N^i \end{array} $$

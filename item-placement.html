Item Placement

Problem Description

Item Placement (IP) involves spreading items, e.g., files or processes, across containers, e.g., disks or machines, utilizing them evenly. Each item has a fixed resource requirement, and each bin has a fixed capacity. The number of items that can be moved is constrained, modeling the real-life situation of a live system for which some placement already exists [Gasse22]. The goal of the problem is to place all items in bins while minimizing the imbalance of the resources used across all bins. The problem is formulated as follows. Let \(I\) denote the set of items and \(J\) denote the set of containers. Let a binary variable \(x_{i j}\) be 1 if item \(i\) is placed in container \(j\) and 0 otherwise. Each item will be placed in exactly a single container. Let \(K\) represent the set of dimensions. \(y_{jk}\) is a variable that specifies the deficit of the utilization of container j in dimension k. $$ \begin{array}{ll} \min _{x, y, z} & \sum_{j \in J} \sum_{k \in K} \alpha_k y_{j k}+\sum_{k \in K} \beta_k z_k \\ \text { s.t. } & \sum_{j \in J} x_{i j}=1 & \forall i \in I \\ & \sum_{i \in I} a_{i k} x_{i j} \leq b_k & \forall j \in J, \forall k \in K\\ & \sum_{i \in I} d_{i k} x_{i j}+y_{j k} \geq 1 & \forall j \in J, \forall k \in K \\ & y_{j k} \leq z_k & \forall j \in J, \forall k \in K\\ & x_{i j} \in\{0,1\} & \forall i \in I, \forall j \in J \\ & y_{j k} \geq 0 & \forall j \in J, \forall k \in K \end{array} $$ $$ $$
